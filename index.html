Understanding the Ford Bronco Roll Cage: What You're Working With

The 2021–2026 Ford Bronco full-size ships with a full structural steel roll cage as a standard feature across every trim level — from the base two-door to the four-door Raptor. Unlike most SUVs where the B-pillar is buried inside door pillars and headliner panels, the Bronco's cage is fully exposed on the interior. Every bar is visible, accessible, and designed to be a mounting point for accessories.

The cage anatomy matters when you're buying accessories. Here's what you're actually working with:

  • A-pillar bars: The front vertical bars at the windshield corners. At head height for front passengers — the primary location for safety foam padding.
  • B-pillar bars: The vertical bars between the front and rear seats. At head height for rear passengers and anyone entering or exiting the rear. Critical for foam padding on trail use.
  • Overhead/top bars: The horizontal cage bars running across the roof interior. Primary mounting surface for overhead MOLLE panels and gear organization platforms.
  • Rear sport bars: The curved bars behind the second-row seats, forming the rear of the cage. Mounting point for chase racks and Hi-Lift jack mounts.
  • Grab handle positions: Typically the B-pillar and rear sport bars — the bars front and rear passengers reach for during off-camber terrain and obstacle crossings.

Full-size Bronco only, all years 2021–2026. The Bronco Sport has no exposed interior roll cage bars — none of the products or recommendations on this page apply to the Sport. The full-size is available in 2-door and 4-door configurations; both have the same full cage. Check your model year and configuration before ordering.

#1 Best Ford Bronco Roll Bar Accessory: Grab Handles

Grab handles for the roll bars are the first upgrade most Bronco owners add after their first trail run — and for good reason. Stock Bronco interiors don't include dedicated grab handles at the rear cage bars. When you're running rock gardens, off-camber ledges, or any terrain where passengers are shifting around in their seats, there's nothing to hold onto except the seat itself. A quality set of roll bar grab handles changes that completely.

The right grab handle for the Bronco is custom-fit — not a universal handle with a shim adapter. The Bronco's roll bar tubes have a specific outer diameter. A handle machined for that exact diameter clamps securely and does not rotate or slide under load. Generic handles sized for "most 1.5–2 inch bars" have enough slop in the clamp to shift during a hard grab — which is exactly when you cannot afford shifting.

Two American brands build Bronco-specific grab handles the right way. We rank Bartact first, Bull Strap second. Both are worth checking before you buy.
